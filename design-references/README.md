# Design references — the drafting language

Source material for the schedule surface's visual language (session 9c in
`SCHEDULE_PLAN.md`). Not part of the app; never served.

Direction: architectural drawing, medieval medical treatises, anatomical
plates, astronomical maps, urban plans. Chloé Vanderstraeten's *Embryon*
series is the closest single reference.

| File | What to take from it |
|---|---|
| `01-urban-plan-guzman.jpg` | Layered translucent plan fragments over white; hard black rules cutting at angles; tiny cyan and red washes against acres of paper; stipple for tonal mass. Composition hangs off a vertical spine. |
| `02-architectural-section.jpg` | Line-weight hierarchy doing all the work. Hatched masonry, ghosted repetition of the wheel through its arc — the model for how recurring events should read. Figures for scale. |
| `03-mechanism-elevation-key.jpg` | Numbered key with leader lines; elevation above, plan below, aligned. One muted ochre on the mechanism and nothing else. The model for block detail. |
| `04-construction-geometry-bartlett.jpg` | Setting-out left visible — projection rays, circles, construction geometry as part of the image. Small studies clustered at the edges. The model for the construction layer. |

**The ratio that matters:** in every one of these the construction geometry is
faint and the subject is dark. Invert it and you get something beautiful that
cannot be read at 8am. The construction layer is texture, never information.
